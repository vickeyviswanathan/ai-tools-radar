The AI Deepfake Video / Sextortion Scam (Deepfake Sextortion) — Modus Operandi & 1930 Protection Guide

Statutory citizen cyber defense advisory based on I4C & State Cyber Police Advisory guidelines.

📱 Attack Vector: Video Call / Social Media 🏷️ Pattern: Deepfake Sextortion 🏛️ Source: I4C & State Cyber Police Advisory
Report at Cybercrime.gov.in ↗ ← View All 31 Scam Alerts

🕵️ How the Scam Works (Modus Operandi)

⚠️ The Trap (Video Call / Social Media): Fraudsters initiate an unsolicited video call from an attractive stranger's profile—or scrape your public LinkedIn/Instagram photos—and use AI face-swap/deepfake tools to fabricate compromising videos. They immediately threaten to send the video to your family, LinkedIn connections, or HR department unless you pay hush money.

🛡️ Mandatory Defense Rule

✅ How to Protect Yourself & Your Family: Never answer unexpected video calls from unknown numbers. Lock down social media friend lists and public profile photos, never pay blackmailers, and preserve screenshots immediately for Cyber Police.

1. Scam Overview

Modern sextortion combines classic video-call recording traps with AI deepfake face-swapping. Even if a victim never appears inappropriately on camera, attackers overlay the victim's face from social media photos onto explicit footage. They target professionals, students, and executives, using LinkedIn and Instagram follower lists as leverage.

2. The Initial Contact Simulated Educational Example

Channel: WhatsApp / Instagram / Facebook Messenger Video Call · Note: The message below is a simulated educational example showing typical wording used by fraudsters.

[Illustrative Educational Example] 11:15 PM: Unknown WhatsApp profile sends a friendly 'Hi' followed within seconds by a direct video call. 11:17 PM (Message after call ends): "Your video is recorded and ready. Look at your Instagram followers / office LinkedIn list—send ₹35,000 in 15 minutes or I upload this to everyone now."

3. How the Scam Works (Step-by-Step)

  1. Reconnaissance: Attackers collect your public profile pictures and identify your family members, friends, or workplace on Facebook, Instagram, or LinkedIn.
  2. The trap or deepfake: Either they lure you into answering a short video call where pre-recorded explicit footage plays alongside your camera feed, or they use AI tools to swap your face onto a fabricated clip without any call.
  3. Rapid extortion: Within minutes, they send a screen recording showing the fabricated video next to screenshots of your contacts' profiles.
  4. Escalating demands: If you pay once, they pretend a 'senior officer' or 'YouTube upload team' needs a second and third payment to delete the file.

4. What the Victim Sees Simulated Screen — Educational Example

Extortion Chat Screen (Simulated Educational Example)
[Forwarded 12-second screen recording showing your face in an AI-altered video] "I already tagged 4 of your relatives and your company HR page. Pay ₹35,000 to UPI [redacted] in 10:00 minutes or I press Send."

Fraudsters often stage fake 'upload in progress' screenshots to create maximum panic within a 10-minute countdown.

5. The Psychology Behind It

Panic, embarrassment, and time pressure. Fear of permanent career or family damage causes rational people to pay before verifying whether the attacker can—or will—actually publish anything.

6. Red Flags to Spot Immediately

  • Late-night or unsolicited video calls from unknown profiles on WhatsApp, Instagram, or Messenger.
  • Immediate blackmail threats featuring a 10-minute countdown and screenshots of your social media follower list.
  • Demands for payment via UPI, gift cards, or crypto to 'delete the video permanently'.

7. What Happens If the Victim Falls for It

  • Repeated financial extortion: Paying once confirms you are willing to pay, leading to endless follow-up demands.
  • Severe emotional distress and anxiety.
  • Possible reputational embarrassment if fabricated media is sent to contacts before accounts are locked down.

8. How to Protect Yourself

  • Enable 'Silence Unknown Callers' in WhatsApp Privacy settings and restrict who can call you on social apps.
  • Make Instagram/Facebook accounts private and hide your friend/follower lists from public view.
  • Never accept video calls from strangers, and keep your camera covered or angled away if answering an unknown call.

9. What to Do If Already Scammed

  1. Do NOT pay a single rupee—payment never buys permanent deletion.
  2. Do NOT delete the chat right away; take clear screenshots of the caller's profile, phone number, threatening messages, and any payment handles.
  3. Lock down your social media privacy settings immediately and temporarily deactivate public profiles if needed.
  4. Block the number after saving evidence, dial 1930, and file a confidential complaint at cybercrime.gov.in (you can also use StopNCII.org to hash/block non-consensual intimate images across participating platforms).

10. India-Specific Reporting Channels

  • National Cyber Crime Portal: File an extortion / morphed content complaint online (anonymous/victim-protected handling). (https://cybercrime.gov.in/)
  • National Cyber Crime Helpline 1930: Call 1930 immediately if you already transferred money. (https://cybercrime.gov.in/)
  • StopNCII.org: Generate a digital hash to help participating platforms block non-consensual intimate or deepfake imagery. (https://stopncii.org/)

🚨 Emergency 4-Step Golden Hour Protocol (If Targeted)

  1. Dial 1930 Immediately (Golden Hour): Call the toll-free National Cyber Crime Helpline 1930 within 2 hours of an unauthorized UPI/bank transfer to freeze the funds in the recipient mule account before ATM withdrawal.
  2. Disconnect Device if an APK Was Installed: If a file ending in .apk (e.g. e-Challan, Wedding Invite, KYC app) was installed, turn on Airplane Mode immediately to stop SMS OTP forwarding, then uninstall the malicious package.
  3. Preserve Uncropped Digital Evidence: Save screenshots of the 12-digit UPI UTR number, bank SMS alerts, WhatsApp/Telegram caller numbers, and chat logs.
  4. File Formal e-FIR & Notify Bank: Submit a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in and hand the 14-digit acknowledgement receipt to your home bank branch within 24 hours.
