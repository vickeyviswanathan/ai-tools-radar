The "Boss Scam" / Executive WhatsApp Takeover Fraud (Boss BEC Scam) — Modus Operandi & 1930 Protection Guide

Statutory citizen cyber defense advisory based on I4C & PIB Cyber Security Advisory guidelines.

📱 Attack Vector: WhatsApp / Corporate Finance 🏷️ Pattern: Boss BEC Scam 🏛️ Source: I4C & PIB Cyber Security Advisory
Report Corporate Fraud: 1930 ↗ 📲 Share on WhatsApp 🖨️ 1-Page Office Poster ← All 34 Alerts

🕵️ How the Scam Works (Modus Operandi)

⚠️ The Trap (WhatsApp / Corporate Finance): Fraudsters infect a company director, CFO, or Chartered Accountant's computer using a malicious archive file (such as Statement_of_Account.zip or RBI_Notice.zip) to hijack their active WhatsApp Web session. Using the executive's real WhatsApp number and chat history, the attackers then order the finance or accounts team to execute urgent high-value vendor or escrow transfers to mule bank accounts.

🛡️ Mandatory Defense Rule

✅ How to Protect Yourself & Your Family: Mandate a strict two-person voice callback rule before releasing any bank transfer requested over WhatsApp or email, never open unexpected .zip/.exe files on work PCs, and audit WhatsApp Linked Devices regularly.

1. Scam Overview

Highlighted by I4C and PIB advisories, the 'Boss Scam' combines infostealer malware with Business Email/Messaging Compromise (BEC). Unlike crude impersonation from unknown numbers, attackers hijack the actual executive's live WhatsApp session—meaning the finance employee sees the real boss's name, photo, phone number, and prior office chat history.

2. The Initial Contact Simulated Educational Example

Channel: Malicious .ZIP to Executive → Live WhatsApp Message to Finance Team · Note: The message below is a simulated educational example showing typical wording used by fraudsters.

[Illustrative Educational Example] From Managing Director's real WhatsApp: "Ramesh, I am in a confidential board/auditor meeting and cannot take calls. Please process an urgent RTGS of ₹18,50,000 to this new vendor settlement account immediately—will share the invoice by 5 PM. Treat as priority."

3. How the Scam Works (Step-by-Step)

  1. Initial compromise: A director, CFO, or CA receives a compressed archive ('Statement of Account.zip', 'MCA_Audit.zip', or 'RBI.zip') via WhatsApp or email and opens the executable inside on a Windows PC.
  2. Session theft: The malware silently exfiltrates active WhatsApp Web / browser session tokens to the attacker's server without logging the executive out.
  3. Reconnaissance: Attackers quietly read recent company finance chats to learn internal payment workflows, vendor names, and the executive's writing tone.
  4. The Boss command: Posing as the executive from their genuine WhatsApp number, the attacker instructs an accountant or finance manager to make an urgent RTGS/IMPS transfer while claiming to be 'in a meeting'.
  5. Rapid layering: Funds land in a mule corporate or savings account and are split across multiple accounts within minutes.

4. What the Victim Sees Simulated Screen — Educational Example

Finance Employee's WhatsApp Chat (Simulated Educational Example)
Contact: Rajesh Sharma (MD — Verified Company Number) "Need ₹18.5L transferred before 3:30 PM RTGS cutoff for confidential acquisition retainer. Send UTR screenshot here once done. Do not call, inside investor briefing."

Because the message comes from the executive's genuine saved contact—with months of real chat history above it—finance staff rarely suspect a takeover.

5. The Psychology Behind It

Workplace authority, urgency, and social proof. Junior and mid-level finance staff hesitate to question an urgent directive from the MD or CFO, especially when told not to disturb them in a meeting.

6. Red Flags to Spot Immediately

  • Urgent high-value transfer or new beneficiary bank account requests sent over WhatsApp with an explicit instruction 'not to call'.
  • Changes to existing vendor bank details or requests to bypass normal ERP/PO approval workflows.
  • An executive's PC recently opening an unexpected .zip/.rar archive or showing an unfamiliar session under WhatsApp → Linked Devices.

7. What Happens If the Victim Falls for It

  • Multi-lakh or multi-crore corporate treasury loss in a single RTGS/NEFT transfer.
  • Exposure of confidential board, tax, and client conversations synced to WhatsApp Web.
  • Operational disruption, audit scrutiny, and vendor payment disputes.

8. How to Protect Yourself

  • Enforce a mandatory out-of-band voice/in-person callback policy for every payment above a set threshold or any new bank beneficiary.
  • Block .zip/.exe/.scr attachments from external WhatsApp/email downloads on corporate finance endpoints.
  • Audit WhatsApp → Settings → Linked Devices weekly on all leadership phones and enable Two-Step Verification.

9. What to Do If Already Scammed

  1. Log out all Linked Devices on the executive's WhatsApp immediately and isolate the affected PC from the corporate network for malware scanning.
  2. Call your corporate bank's relationship manager and fraud desk immediately to recall/freeze the RTGS/NEFT/IMPS transfer.
  3. Dial 1930 within the Golden Hour and file a formal corporate cybercrime report at cybercrime.gov.in with UTR numbers and bank logs.
  4. Preserve the malicious .zip file and PC logs for CERT-In / Cyber Police forensics.

10. India-Specific Reporting Channels

  • National Cyber Crime Helpline 1930 & Portal: Report immediately with UTR numbers to freeze recipient mule accounts. (https://cybercrime.gov.in/)
  • CERT-In Incident Response: Report corporate malware infection and executive account compromise. (https://www.cert-in.org.in/)
  • Corporate Bank Fraud Desk: Request an immediate SWIFT/RTGS/NEFT beneficiary freeze.

🚨 Emergency 4-Step Golden Hour Protocol (If Targeted)

  1. Dial 1930 Immediately (Golden Hour): Call the toll-free National Cyber Crime Helpline 1930 within 2 hours of an unauthorized UPI/bank transfer to freeze the funds in the recipient mule account before ATM withdrawal.
  2. Disconnect Device if an APK Was Installed: If a file ending in .apk (e.g. e-Challan, Wedding Invite, KYC app) was installed, turn on Airplane Mode immediately to stop SMS OTP forwarding, then uninstall the malicious package.
  3. Preserve Uncropped Digital Evidence: Save screenshots of the 12-digit UPI UTR number, bank SMS alerts, WhatsApp/Telegram caller numbers, and chat logs.
  4. File Formal e-FIR & Notify Bank: Submit a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in and hand the 14-digit acknowledgement receipt to your home bank branch within 24 hours.
