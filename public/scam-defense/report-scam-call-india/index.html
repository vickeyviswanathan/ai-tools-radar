How to Report a Scam Call in India: Chakshu and 1930

Choose the correct reporting channel, preserve useful evidence and avoid follow-up scams.

Published 13 November 2026 · Updated 13 November 2026 · By Cipherati
Report at cybercrime.gov.in ↗ Call 1930 ← All scam alerts & playbooks

The right reporting channel depends on one question: have you lost money?

  • No money lost: Report a suspected fraud call, SMS or WhatsApp communication through Sanchar Saathi’s Chakshu facility.
  • Money lost: Call 1930 and report through cybercrime.gov.in. Contact your bank or payment provider through an official channel as well.

Do not delay a financial-fraud report while trying to complete every other step.

When to use Chakshu

Chakshu facilitates reports of suspected fraud communications intended for cybercrime, financial fraud, impersonation or other misuse through calls, SMS or WhatsApp. Its official page lists examples such as bank, payment-wallet, SIM, gas, electricity and KYC-update impersonation.

Chakshu instructs users to report suspected fraud communications within 30 days for action. Reports made later may still help identify spammers, but you should not treat that as a reason to wait.

Use Chakshu for a suspicious communication even when you did not pay. Your report may contribute to wider prevention work.

When to bypass Chakshu and act on the financial loss

The official Chakshu page explicitly says that if you have already lost money, you should report at 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in.

Also contact your bank, card issuer or payment provider using details from its official app, website or your account documents. Save every complaint number.

Financial fraud can involve more than a completed transfer. Treat an unauthorised collect request, card charge or wallet debit as a financial incident. If credentials were exposed, follow the provider’s security instructions immediately.

Information to preserve

Before blocking or deleting, securely record:

  • Caller or sender number
  • Date, time and communication channel
  • The name or organisation the sender claimed to represent
  • Exact message content and relevant screenshots
  • Links, attachments, APK files or app names involved
  • UPI ID, bank account or payment destination shown by the sender
  • Transaction details and acknowledgement numbers, if money was lost

Do not open a suspicious attachment again to create a better screenshot. Do not forward malware files to friends or family as a warning; describe the message without passing on the dangerous payload.

What not to do

  • Do not call back repeatedly or provoke the sender.
  • Do not share an OTP, PIN, password or identity document to “verify” the complaint.
  • Do not install an app at the caller’s request.
  • Do not click a reporting link supplied by the suspected scammer.
  • Do not publish someone’s full bank details, identity documents or private conversation publicly.
  • Do not pay anyone who guarantees recovery or promises to remove your number from a list.

Use official websites by typing the known address yourself or using a trusted bookmark.

After reporting

Block the sender through your device or platform when appropriate. Keep the original evidence and complaint references. Monitor financial accounts if you disclosed payment details or interacted with a transaction request.

Warn vulnerable family members using the pattern rather than alarming them with a long list of brand names:

An unsolicited caller created urgency, claimed authority and asked for secrecy, payment or credentials. End the call and verify independently.

If the same contact attempts continue, preserve the new details and use the appropriate official reporting channel again.

Quick decision guide

SituationFirst action
Suspicious call or message; no payment or lossReport through Chakshu within 30 days
Money transferred, debited or stolenContact the provider, call 1930 and report at cybercrime.gov.in
Credentials or remote access exposedSecure the account/device with the relevant provider and report any loss
Someone offers guaranteed recovery for a feeDisengage and do not pay

Reporting should be boring, documented and official. Fear and urgency belong to the scam; they do not need to control your response.

This guide provides general safety information, not legal advice or a promise of recovery. Always confirm current procedures on the official websites linked above.
