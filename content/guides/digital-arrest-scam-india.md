# Digital Arrest Scam in India: Spot It and Respond

A “digital arrest” call is designed to make you obey before you have time to verify. The caller may claim to be from the police, a telecom regulator, customs, a court or an investigative agency. The story often involves a parcel, Aadhaar, a mobile connection, money laundering or another serious allegation.

The decisive response is simple: **end the call, contact the claimed institution through an independently verified channel, and do not send money or credentials.**

## What the scam looks like

The script can change, but the pressure pattern is familiar:

1. **Authority:** The caller uses an official title, uniform, logo, forged notice or staged office background.
2. **Accusation:** You are linked to a crime, suspicious parcel, SIM offence or frozen account.
3. **Escalation:** The call moves to another “officer” or a video platform.
4. **Isolation:** You are told not to contact family, a lawyer or your local police station.
5. **Surveillance:** You may be instructed to keep your camera on or remain continuously available.
6. **Extraction:** The caller demands a fine, security deposit, verification payment, banking details, OTP or device access.

A [TRAI advisory published by the Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2153153) warns that callers impersonate TRAI or law-enforcement officials, use forged documents and threats, and coerce transfers under pretexts such as bail, fines or verification. TRAI states that no regulatory body conducts investigations or collects payments through phone calls, messaging apps or video platforms.

## The red flags that matter

Treat the interaction as unsafe when a caller combines an official accusation with any of these demands:

- Stay on the call and do not contact anyone
- Move to a video call for an immediate investigation
- Pay to avoid arrest, clear a case or verify funds
- Transfer money to a “safe” or “verification” account
- Share Aadhaar, bank details, card details, passwords or OTPs
- Install an app or grant remote access
- Keep the supposed investigation secret

A uniform, official-looking document or video background does not resolve the doubt. Digital material can be fabricated.

## What to do during the call

**Do not argue and do not try to prove your innocence during the call.** Say nothing further that reveals your identity, finances or location. End it.

If you can do so safely, preserve the number, time, call logs, messages, profile names and any documents sent to you. Do not remain engaged merely to collect better evidence.

Contact a trusted person. Isolation helps the scammer; a second person can help you slow down and verify.

Then find the claimed agency’s contact details independently through its official website or trusted documents. Do not use a number or link supplied by the caller.

## What to do if you already paid

Follow the financial-fraud response steps immediately:

- Contact your bank or payment provider through an official channel.
- Call **1930** for online financial-fraud assistance.
- Report at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/).
- Preserve transaction IDs, recipient details, screenshots and complaint numbers.

Prompt reporting can help the response process, but no honest guide can promise recovery. Do not pay a self-described recovery agent who appears afterwards.

## What to do if no money was lost

If you received a suspected fraud communication by call, SMS or WhatsApp, you can report it through the Government’s [Sanchar Saathi Chakshu facility](https://sancharsaathi.gov.in/sfc). Chakshu states that suspected fraud communications should be reported within 30 days for action. If money has already been lost, its guidance directs you to **1930** or **cybercrime.gov.in** instead.

## How to talk to a family member who is being targeted

Do not begin with ridicule or blame. Fear and shame can keep a victim engaged with the caller.

Use a direct interruption:

> End the call. You do not have to decide or pay anything now. We will verify through an official number together.

Help them secure the account, preserve evidence and report. If they are distressed, remain with them and involve another trusted person or qualified professional as needed.

## Remember this rule

A caller who claims authority must still be verified independently. Urgency, secrecy and payment demands are not proof of an investigation; they are reasons to stop.
