# UPI Fraud: What to Do Immediately in India

If money has left your account because of a UPI scam, act now. Contact your bank or payment app, call **1930**, and report the incident through the [National Cybercrime Reporting Portal](https://cybercrime.gov.in/). Fast reporting may help authorities and financial institutions act on the transaction, but recovery is not guaranteed.

Do not pay anyone who contacts you afterwards and promises to recover the money for a fee. That can be a second scam.

## 1. Stop further losses

- Stop communicating with the suspected scammer.
- Do not approve more collect requests, scan more QR codes or share another OTP.
- If you entered your UPI PIN or banking credentials after being manipulated, contact your bank or payment provider immediately and follow its account-security process.
- If you installed an app or granted screen-sharing access during the incident, disconnect the device from sensitive activity and seek qualified technical help before accessing financial accounts again.
- Save evidence before blocking the sender or deleting messages.

Do not attempt to confront, threaten or trap the scammer. Your priority is securing the account and reporting the transaction.

## 2. Contact your bank or payment provider

Use the support option inside the official app or a number obtained from the provider’s official website or your bank documents. Do not trust a customer-care number copied from an unsolicited message, social-media comment or unverified search advertisement.

Explain that the transaction was fraudulent. Ask the provider to record the complaint and tell you what additional steps are required. If the transaction was made through UPI, the [NPCI dispute-redressal mechanism](https://www.npci.org.in/what-we-do/upi/dispute-redressal-mechanism) explains that complaints can be raised through the payment service provider or third-party UPI app, including for fraudulent transactions.

Record the complaint reference number, date, time and name of the support channel you used.

## 3. Call 1930 and report online

India’s cybercrime helpline **1930** assists victims of online financial fraud with prompt reporting. You can also file through [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/). The Government of India describes both channels as part of its citizen financial cyber-fraud reporting system.

Provide accurate information. Do not exaggerate the loss or omit a second transaction. Save the acknowledgement or complaint number you receive and use it for follow-up.

## 4. Preserve this evidence

Create a secure folder containing:

- Transaction ID or UPI reference number
- Amount, date and exact time
- Your debited account or wallet details
- Recipient UPI ID, bank details or merchant information, if visible
- Screenshots of the transaction and confirmation messages
- Phone number, UPI ID, profile name and messages used by the suspected scammer
- Relevant emails, links, QR codes, app names or remote-access details
- Bank complaint reference and cybercrime acknowledgement numbers

Keep original files where possible. Screenshots are useful, but they do not replace transaction records from your provider.

## 5. Secure your broader digital identity

If you revealed a password, change it on the official service and anywhere else it was reused. Use a unique password and enable the strongest available multi-factor authentication. If you shared identity documents, card details or other sensitive information, ask the relevant provider what monitoring or protective steps apply.

Watch your statements and account alerts. Report additional unauthorised transactions separately and connect them to the original complaint where appropriate.

## Why a QR code or UPI PIN request is a warning sign

According to [NPCI’s UPI safety guidance](https://www.npci.org.in/safety-feature), you enter a UPI PIN only when money is deducted from your account; a UPI PIN is **not required to receive money**. NPCI’s [fraud-awareness guidance](https://www.npci.org.in/fraud-awareness) likewise warns that scanning a QR code and entering a UPI PIN is for making payments, not receiving them.

A stranger who says you must scan a code or enter your PIN to receive a refund, prize, salary, rent payment or marketplace payment is giving you a reason to stop.

## What not to believe afterwards

Be wary of anyone who:

- Guarantees recovery of the stolen money
- Demands an advance “release,” “legal,” or “refund” fee
- Claims to be from 1930 or a bank but contacts you through an unofficial account
- Asks for an OTP, UPI PIN, screen-sharing session or remote-access installation
- Pressures you to keep the recovery process secret

Verify claims through the official institution, not through contact details supplied by the caller.

## A simple response checklist

1. Stop payments and communication.
2. Secure the affected account and device.
3. Contact the bank or payment provider through an official channel.
4. Call 1930 and report at cybercrime.gov.in.
5. Save complaint numbers and all transaction evidence.
6. Ignore unsolicited recovery offers.
